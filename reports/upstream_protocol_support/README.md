## Findings that are not in the earlier feasibility document (all verified in the tables above)
1. **Upstream gauges report at 5-minute stamps in recent years.** 24 of the 135 gauge-event series with data (6 events: 2019-05-08, 2021-10-14, 2024-07-24, 2025-07-05, 2026-06-15, 2026-07-15) have a median step of 5 min, the others 15 min (target gauge 08167500: 15 min in a one-request check for 2025-07-03/04). The original analysis assumed 15-min spacing: its coverage statistic (non-missing count divided by the number of 15-min intervals) can exceed 1 for these series (stored coverage values of 1.28 to 2.29), which hides gaps, and its "3-hour rise" (`shift(12)`) is a 1-hour rise on 5-min data. Re-running the same code on the 15-min grid (`S8`): in the wider v2 windows 3 gauge-events change status to "insufficient coverage" (Hunt 2025-07-05, Comfort 2026-06-15, Johnson Ck 2026-07-15) and in the narrower v3 windows used for the dropout flags in `S1` two do (Hunt 2025-07-05, Comfort 2026-06-15), single-gauge leads change by up to 30 h (Comfort 2026-07-15, from -9.2 h to +20.8 h), best-lead values per event change by at most 1.4 h wherever the stored tables had a value (largest: v2 for 2026-07-15, 19.33 to 18.00) and **no event changes class**. The earlier document's headline range (6 to 8 of 18 events with a lead >= 6 h) is therefore not changed by this issue, but its dropout and per-gauge statements for those 6 events should be read with this correction.
2. **Four gauge-events counted as "no data" were failed downloads, not outages.** The original fetch swallowed errors and wrote no file. A public NWIS re-query returned complete series for Johnson Ck (2016-05-29), and Bergheim (2018-09-09, 2025-07-05, 2026-07-15) (`S6b`). Recomputed leads for them: Johnson Ck 2016-05-29 +16.25 h; Bergheim 2018-09-09 -7.00 h; 2025-07-05 +16.25 h but with inconsistent timings; 2026-07-15 +8.00 h (v2, consistent) or +15.50 h (v3, inconsistent). No event changes class.
3. **The hourly radar+gauge product changes name between 2018 and 2021** in the Iowa State archive (`GaugeCorr_QPE_01H` for the probed 2015-2019 dates, `MultiSensor_QPE_01H_Pass2` for the probed 2021-2026 dates; the `mrms` folder exists on both). The first probed date of the earlier product is 2015-05-06 (4 files that day; absent 2015-05-04); the product was absent on 2015-04-23 and 2015-05-04 (`mrms_archive_probe.csv`), later than the "late 2014" start suggested by the monthly probe in the feasibility document, which looked at folder presence rather than at this product. Whether the two products are statistically homogeneous is NOT verified. 2012-01-25 and 2013-10-31 have no hourly product (an `nmq` folder exists for 2012-01-24/25 only).
4. **Fold geometry limits rainfall-input experiments.** Because each fold's training period ends before its validation chunk, the hourly QPE record (from 2015-05-06) lies inside the training period of only folds 4, 5 and 6 (0.65, 2.65 and 7.65 years); folds 1 to 3 have none (`S5`).
5. Proposed event classes (rule in the protocol, section 3.6): **6 upstream-led (5 storm clusters), 6 local-or-downstream-led (6 clusters), 6 ambiguous (5 clusters)**; the class counts are identical whether computed from the stored tables or from the 15-min-grid recomputation (`S1`, column `class_from_stored_tables`).
6. Documentation inconsistency for the project owner (existing documents not edited here): `docs/GUIDE_BRIEF.md` and `docs/UPSTREAM_INPUT_FEASIBILITY.md` (sections 4.2 and the guide brief, decision 3) still speak of a "92-station" gauge product, while `reports/gauge_rainfall/README.md` reports 187 stations inside the basin polygon and the per-event column `gauge_daily_n_stations_inside_that_day` of `S1` shows 58 to 77 reporting inside the basin on the key days.

## Limits of these tables
Timing leads are descriptive and depend on a 1 ft / 3 h trigger and a 6 h consistency tolerance; the classes describe the response at upstream *gauges*, not where rain fell; archive probes prove only that index pages list files, not that files are complete or usable; NWIS values are the archived record, not as-received telemetry; no discharge series was downloaded.
