# Phase 1E addendum: onset-timing comparison, GRU 24 h versus 72 h (specified BEFORE the analysis was run)

Written 2026-11-22. Experiment id **E18**. Output folder `reports/phase1e_onset/` (new), report `reports/PHASE1E_ONSET_TIMING.md`. Inputs are only the saved Phase 1E out-of-fold predictions (`reports/phase1e/oof_predictions.csv.gz`, three seeds per window) and, as references, the saved Phase 1D predictions (`reports/phase1d/oof_predictions.csv.gz`: persistence, damped trend, ridge H). **No model is retrained; no Phase 1C, 1D or 1E artifact is modified.**

## 0. Status and integrity
This is a **post-hoc diagnostic**: it was requested after the Phase 1E numbers were known (the Phase 1E decision, 24 h retained, is already made by the pre-registered rule and is not reopened here). Everything is retrospective blocked cross-validation, exploratory, 18 events from 14 storm clusters. The definitions below were fixed before the onset analysis was run. Nothing here is a warning-capability claim, and no result will be read from peak-capture ratios alone.

## 1. Events and definitions (all existing; nothing new is defined)
- **Events:** the 18 evaluated >= 10 ft events of Phase 1D/1E (`experimental_stage_10ft`, event start `i0` = first sample >= 10 ft, folds 1-6). Official NWS events (>= 25 ft) are flagged: 5 rows, because the >= 10 ft catalogue merges the two official 2026 events. Rapid-rise events (rise >= 4 ft in 3 h, 25 events from 22 clusters) are used only in section 3 (fixed-rule alarm leads).
- **Rise onset `o`:** `events.rise_onset_index(stage, i0)` (the last sample within 0.5 ft of the preceding-72 h minimum before `i0`; the same quantity that starts the Phase 1D/1E flood window 6 h earlier). **Peak** `peak_i`, **baseline** `b` = minimum stage in the 72 h before `i0`, **amplitude** `A = peak - b`.
- **Horizons** 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 3 h; all four are reported, 1 h and 3 h are the focus (the 15-min time grid cannot resolve a shift smaller than one step, 15 min).
- A forecast **issued** at time `t` for horizon `h` is a forecast of the stage at the **target time** `t + h`. "Forecast series at target time" `F_h[j]` is the forecast issued at `j - h`.
- **Variants:** GRU 24 h seeds 0, 1, 2 and GRU 72 h seeds 0, 1, 2 (Phase 1E); references from Phase 1D: persistence (zero anticipation by construction), damped trend (extrapolates an ongoing rise), ridge H. Seed spread is the noise yardstick: a 72 h versus 24 h difference is only noted as a difference if it exceeds the range across the three 24 h seeds.

## 2. Onset-timing metrics (per event, per horizon, per variant)
**2.1 Level-arrival timing (does the forecast reach a level before the river does?).** Levels `L_f = b + f*A` for `f` = 0.1, 0.5, 0.9 (start of the rise, half of the rise, near the peak). Observed arrival `T_O(f)`: first sample in `[o, peak_i]` with stage >= `L_f`. Forecast arrival `T_F(f)`: first target time in `[o - 6 h, peak_i + h + 2 h]` at which the forecast series at target time is >= `L_f` (finite values only; a forecast that never reaches the level is reported as "not reached" and is never imputed). **Lag** = `T_F - T_O` (hours; positive = the forecast shows the level later than the river reached it). **Lead** = `h - lag` = observed arrival time minus the issue time of the first forecast that reaches the level (positive = that forecast was issued before the river reached the level; zero = the forecast only followed the river, as persistence does; at most `h` for an exact forecast). **Catch-up** = lag at 10% minus lag at 90% (positive = the forecast starts late and then closes the gap near the peak).
**2.2 Pre-onset anticipation (genuine anticipation, as opposed to extrapolating a rise that has already started).** Qualifying issue times `i` in `[o - 6 h, o]` with stage at `i` <= `b + 0.5 ft` (the river has not yet risen). Predicted change `d_hat = F_issue[i] - stage[i]`. Per event, horizon and variant: maximum `d_hat` over qualifying issue times; **anticipation flag** = maximum `d_hat` >= 0.5 ft (the same 0.5 ft tolerance that defines `o`; sensitivity at 0.25 ft and 1.0 ft is reported); issue time and lead (relative to `o` and to `i0`) of the first qualifying issue time with `d_hat` >= 0.5 ft; the realised change over the same horizon at that issue time, for context. **Background rate:** share of issue times outside every >= 10 ft flood window and every rapid-rise window with `d_hat` >= 0.5 ft, per variant and horizon, to say how often the same flag would fire for no event. A model that only extrapolates an ongoing rise cannot produce a pre-onset flag.
**2.3 Peak timing:** target time of the maximum of the forecast series in the window minus the observed peak time (hours), and forecast maximum divided by observed peak (target-aligned; differs from the Phase 1D/1E peak ratio, which uses the forecast issued `h` before the peak).

## 3. Fixed-rule alarm lead (existing detection definition)
The existing alarm rule, hysteresis and matching (`experiment.fixed_rule_raw`, `experiment.detect`, tolerance 1 h) applied to the saved predictions for the >= 10 ft stage definition (18 events) and the rise >= 4 ft/3 h definition (25 events): per event and seed, status (early / late / miss / not evaluable) and lead. Reproduction check: the hit counts must equal `reports/phase1e/tables/E6b_detection_seed_summary.csv`; if they do not, the analysis stops and the discrepancy is reported.

## 4. Events where onset timing cannot be calculated reliably (flags fixed in advance)
(R1) fewer than 80% of target times in `[o, peak_i + h]` have a finite forecast (per horizon and variant); (R2) more than 5% of observed samples missing in `[o - 6 h, peak_i]`; (R3) **ambiguous onset**: `peak_i - o` > 24 h (a slow, multi-stage rise; the onset is not a single event; onset-based quantities 2.2 and the 10% level are flagged, the 50% and 90% levels remain defined); (R4) amplitude `A` < 3 ft; (R5) provisional data in the event (informational). Flagged events stay in the tables with the flag; summaries are shown with and without flagged events.

## 5. Interpretation rules (fixed in advance)
"The 72 h window changes response behaviour" would be stated only if, at 1 h or 3 h, (a) the sign of the per-event 72 h minus 24 h lead difference at the 10% and 50% levels is the same in at least 12 of the 18 events and the median difference exceeds the 24 h seed range, or (b) the number of events with a pre-onset anticipation flag differs by more than the spread across seeds. Otherwise the conclusion is "no evidence that it changes response behaviour". Descriptive only: no p-values (18 events, 14 clusters), no pooling across horizons, no claim from peak ratios.

## Deviations
1. (Logged after the first run.) The first run of `scripts/run_onset_timing.py` showed that the pre-specified 0.5 ft anticipation flag (section 2.2) does not separate anticipation from reaction to the first uptick (the damped trend flags 8 of 18 events at 3 h). I therefore ADDED, without changing any pre-specified definition, flag or threshold: the issue time of the first forecast that shows each level relative to the rise onset (`issue_vs_onset_*` columns, table `O3c`), the official-events table `O8`, and the fixed-rule lead summary `O7d`. The first-run outputs were overwritten by the rerun of the same code plus these additions. Figure axes were clipped (events with an ambiguous onset give leads of 10 to 30 h) and two-line titles added after viewing the first figures.
2. Interpretation rule (section 5) outcome: neither criterion (a) nor (b) is met; see `reports/PHASE1E_ONSET_TIMING.md`.
